# Response to the Associate Editor and Reviewers — Minor Revision

**Manuscript:** 5125369, *FlowMOP: An Automated Flow Cytometry Time, Debris, and Doublet Removal Tool*

We thank the Editor-in-Chief, the Associate Editor, and the reviewers for their positive assessment of the revised manuscript. We have made the requested minor revisions. Each comment is reproduced below and followed by our response and the revised text. In the tracked manuscript, blue text was added and red struck-through text was removed; it shows only the changes made in this revision.

## Associate Editor

### Associate Editor, Comment 1

> "acquired erroneously" - the erroneously modifies the word acquired. Meaning there was an error in the acquisition process. I think the authors mean the data had some sort of error.

**Response:** We agree. We meant that the recorded measurements of these events may be compromised, not that the acquisition process itself was in error. We revised the sentence in the Introduction:

> “I) Time gating: operators perform time-gating to remove events whose measurements may be compromised by artifacts in the instrument or sample, including air bubbles, blockages, or laser malfunctions.”

### Associate Editor, Comment 2

> "fluorescence measurements lack reliability". The fluorescent measurement is perfectly reliable, it is just a measure of 2 cells instead of 1.

**Response:** We agree. We revised the sentence in the Introduction:

> “III) Doublet gating: Events classified as "doublets"—two or more cells erroneously detected as one—are removed because their fluorescence measurements represent the combined signal of two or more cells rather than a single cell.”

### Associate Editor, Comment 3

> "Peak strength" - should be peak height, or intensity, as used later in the paragraph.

**Response:** We agree and now use “peak height”. We revised the sentence in the Introduction:

> “Doublets feature a signal peak height comparable to that of a single cell, but for twice the duration.”

## Reviewer 2

### Reviewer 2, Comment 3

> Just some unrelated complains. I can tell that the authors use AI to assist the revision. I am not against the use of AI. However, the authors should carefully check the words generated by AI. For example, I am not very sure what this sentence means “FlowCut and PeacoQC provide the most direct comparison for its time-gating component.” (counterparts?) Sometimes, AI can polish too literary and keeps changing the choice of words even for key concepts and make it difficult to follow in a scientific article. Another example is “It defines a “microblockage” operationally as a short, self-resolving mid-acquisition disturbance that produces a localized fluorescence shift”. What do these adjectives mean here? What is “self-resolving”? What is “localized” ? These fancy words do not have enough context to support easy understanding and can lead to confusion.

**Response:** We thank the reviewer for this comment and agree that both sentences were unclear. We rewrote the comparator sentence with the reviewer's suggested meaning. We also replaced the adjectives “self-resolving” and “localized” with a plain description of what they meant. “Self-resolving” meant that the disturbance ends without intervention. “Localized” meant that the fluorescence shift affects only the events acquired during the short disturbance. 

### Changes made

- We revised the comparator sentence in the Introduction:

  > “FlowCut and PeacoQC are the closest existing counterparts to FlowMOP’s time-gating component, so we used them as its comparators.”

- We revised the definition of “microblockage” in the Introduction:

  > “Here, we use “microblockage” to denote a brief mid-acquisition disturbance that ends by itself and shifts the fluorescence intensities of only the events acquired during that interval.”

- The Methods (“Computational construction of Segment, Bimix, and Trimix datasets”) and the Discussion (“Synthetic Sample Time Gating”) no longer repeat the definition. They use the term as defined in the Introduction. In the Discussion, the term now joins the next sentence:

  > “The Bimix and Trimix samples were designed to represent microblockages.”
